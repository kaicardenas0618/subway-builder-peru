## Additional Features

* **Population Grid** — Resident demand is generated from census-block/manzana-level population data and aggregated into 200-meter hexagonal cells.
* **Workplace Grid** — Workplace demand is generated from VCENEC 2022 economic-census employment data and aggregated into the same grid.
* **Special Demand** — Major universities, malls, markets, attractions, hospitals, terminals, stadiums, and the airport are modled in the map.
* **Municipal Coverage** — The map covers Lima Province and the Constitutional Province of Callao.
* **OSRM Commute Modeling** — The demand generation process uses OSRM road routing to estimate driving distance and travel time between generated residence and job pairs.

## Methodology

This map uses a two-stage demand model.

First, Lima + Callao are divided into a 200-meter hexagonal grid. Resident demand is allocated from manzana-level population data into hexes. Job demand is allocated from VCENEC 2022 workplace/economic-census data into the same grid. The result is a base grid where each cell contains `RESIDENTS` and `JOBS`.

Second, the demand generator converts the grid into Subway Builder demand points. Each grid cell becomes a base point with residential capacity and workplace demand. Additional special demand points are added for major trip generators that are not fully represented by the base employment grid, such as Jorge Chávez International Airport, Gamarra, major shopping centers, universities, hospitals, stadiums, attractions, and intercity bus terminal clusters.

## Data Sources

* **Population** — Manzana-level 2017 census population shapefiles for Lima Province and Callao, using `T_TOTAL` as resident population.
* **Workplaces / Jobs** — Peru VCENEC 2022 economic-census data, aggregated to manzana-level workplace/job demand.
* **Administrative Boundaries** — INEI district and manzana boundary shapefiles.
* **Special Demand** — Curated major-destination list including airport, universities, markets, malls, hospitals, attractions, stadiums, and bus terminals. Values are modeled as daily commuter-equivalent destination demand using a mix of sourced public figures and conservative planning estimates.
* **Routing** — OSRM road network routing, when available, for generated commute distances and travel times.

* **Regional Coverage** — The map covers Arequipa metro, Caylloma/Colca tourism corridor, and Islay/Mollendo/Matarani coast.
* **Mixed-Resolution Rural Aggregation** — Expanded regional areas can preserve dense urban detail while grouping sparse rural demand into larger cells for playability.

## Methodology

This map uses a multi-stage demand model.

First, the configured region is divided into a base hexagonal grid. Resident demand is allocated from manzana-level population data into hexes. Job demand is allocated from VCENEC 2022 workplace/economic-census data into the same grid. For expanded regional maps, sparse rural cells may be aggregated into larger cells while urban and town centers retain finer detail. The result is a base grid where each cell contains `RESIDENTS` and `JOBS`.

Second, the demand generator converts the grid into Subway Builder demand points. Each grid cell becomes a base point with residential capacity and workplace demand. Additional special demand points are added for major trip generators that are not fully represented by the base employment grid, such as airports, ports, universities, markets, shopping centers, tourism hubs, hospitals, terminals, and industrial areas.

* **Routing** — OSRM road network routing, when available, for generated commute distances and travel times.

## Additional Features

* **Population Grid** — Resident demand is generated from census-block/manzana-level population data and aggregated into a mixed 400-meter / 1-kilometer hexagonal grid, with a 200-meter refined urban output where generated.
* **Workplace Grid** — Workplace demand is generated from VCENEC 2022 economic-census employment data and aggregated into the same demand grid.
* **Special Demand** — Major universities, malls, markets, attractions, hospitals, terminals, airports/ports, tourism hubs, and industrial centers are modeled in the map.
* **Regional Coverage** — The map covers Piura/Sullana corridor, Paita port, Talara coast, Sechura, and Chulucanas/Morropón inland demand.
* **Mixed-Resolution Rural Aggregation** — Expanded regional areas can preserve dense urban detail while grouping sparse rural demand into larger cells for playability.
* **OSRM Commute Modeling** — The demand generation process uses OSRM road routing, when available, to estimate driving distance and travel time between generated residence and job pairs.

## Methodology

This map uses a multi-stage demand model.

First, the configured region is divided into a base hexagonal grid. Resident demand is allocated from manzana-level population data into hexes. Job demand is allocated from VCENEC 2022 workplace/economic-census data into the same grid. For expanded regional maps, sparse rural cells may be aggregated into larger cells while urban and town centers retain finer detail. The result is a base grid where each cell contains `RESIDENTS` and `JOBS`.

Second, the demand generator converts the grid into Subway Builder demand points. Each grid cell becomes a base point with residential capacity and workplace demand. Additional special demand points are added for major trip generators that are not fully represented by the base employment grid, such as airports, ports, universities, markets, shopping centers, tourism hubs, hospitals, terminals, and industrial areas.

## Data Sources

* **Population** — GeoGPS/INEI-derived 2017 manzana-level population shapefiles from the shared `data/raw/population/extracted/` folder, filtered to the configured province scope and using `T_TOTAL` as resident population.
* **Workplaces / Jobs** — Peru VCENEC 2022 economic-census data, aggregated to manzana-level workplace/job demand.
* **Administrative Boundaries** — INEI district, province, and manzana boundary shapefiles.
* **Special Demand** — Curated major-destination list including airports/ports, universities, markets, malls, hospitals, attractions, terminals, and industrial or agroindustrial centers. Values are modeled as daily commuter-equivalent destination demand using conservative planning estimates.
* **Routing** — OSRM road network routing, when available, for generated commute distances and travel times.

## Additional Features

* **Regional Coverage** — The map covers Chimbote/Santa, Casma, Huarmey, port-industrial coastline, and coastal tourism nodes.
* **Mixed-Resolution Rural Aggregation** — Expanded regional areas can preserve dense urban detail while grouping sparse rural demand into larger cells for playability.
* **OSRM Commute Modeling** — The demand generation process uses OSRM road routing, when available, to estimate driving distance and travel time between generated residence and job pairs.

## Methodology

This map uses a multi-stage demand model.

First, the configured region is divided into a base hexagonal grid. Resident demand is allocated from manzana-level population data into hexes. Job demand is allocated from VCENEC 2022 workplace/economic-census data into the same grid. For expanded regional maps, sparse rural cells may be aggregated into larger cells while urban and town centers retain finer detail. The result is a base grid where each cell contains `RESIDENTS` and `JOBS`.

Second, the demand generator converts the grid into Subway Builder demand points. Each grid cell becomes a base point with residential capacity and workplace demand. Additional special demand points are added for major trip generators that are not fully represented by the base employment grid, such as airports, ports, universities, markets, shopping centers, tourism hubs, hospitals, terminals, and industrial areas.

## Data Sources

* **Workplaces / Jobs** — Peru VCENEC 2022 economic-census data, aggregated to manzana-level workplace/job demand.
* **Administrative Boundaries** — INEI district, province, and manzana boundary shapefiles.
* **Special Demand** — Curated major-destination list including airports/ports, universities, markets, malls, hospitals, attractions, terminals, and industrial or agroindustrial centers. Values are modeled as daily commuter-equivalent destination demand using conservative planning estimates.
* **Routing** — OSRM road network routing, when available, for generated commute distances and travel times.
